Privacy

Last updated 2026-11-25. Operator and contact details: to be added by the project owner.

In short

  • The Nex checks files on your computer. Your files are never uploaded.
  • You do not need an account.
  • No cookies, no ads and no visitor statistics, on the website or in the app.

This website

  • It stores nothing on your computer, so there is no cookie banner. It contacts no other company unless you send a problem report.
  • The website runs on Vercel. Vercel sees your IP address (your internet connection's number) and keeps normal visit logs.
  • The link in a confirmation email contains a one-time code. This page reads it only to choose which message to show you, then removes it. It is not stored or sent.

Problem reports

  • The report form sends only what you type, and only when you press Send: the kind of problem, your description, details you pasted, your email if you gave one, and the app version if the app opened the page. It goes to Supabase, an online storage service, which also sees your IP address.
  • To stop spam (10 reports a day), we keep a scrambled code made from your IP address for about a day. During that time the project owner could link it to your address. It is kept apart from your report.
  • Only the project owner can read reports. They are kept until the owner deletes them.
  • The app never sends a report by itself. "Report a problem" and "Report as safe" only open the form. "Report as safe" also copies details for you to paste: the kind of threat, its score, the reasons, and the file's name and fingerprint (a code that identifies its exact contents). It leaves out the folder and removes your account name from any other path.

What the app looks at

  • All the time: new and changed files in the Downloads and Desktop folders of every account and in temporary folders (on a Mac, only the shared /tmp), and the program file, location and start command of every running program.
  • In a quick or scheduled scan: everyone's Downloads and Desktop.
  • In a full scan, which you start: every account's files and the places programs are installed. On Windows that is the whole main drive.
  • When you open the startup list: what starts with the computer.
  • It puts hidden decoy files in Desktop and Documents to notice ransomware.

Folders it must leave alone

  • In versions after 0.4.1 you can list folders and files it must not open, move or delete (Settings, "Never scan"). Changing the list needs an administrator's approval and is noted in the event log on your computer.
  • Two things still happen. The location and start command of running programs are still looked at. And if the decoy files point to a program in a listed place, its maker's signature is read and it can be stopped.
  • A file you drop on the window is always checked, because you asked.

What stays on your computer

  • Settings, the event log (with file locations and fingerprints), files in quarantine and the last scan results.
  • Your sign-in session, a device number, and how you set up the app's look. The session is removed when you sign out. Your password is never stored.
  • AegisMesh is a simulation. Camera pictures are used on your computer and are never recorded or sent.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional. If you do, Supabase receives and stores:

  • Your email, a scrambled form of your password, and sign-in logs with your IP address and app type.
  • About once a minute, a summary of your computer: its number and name, its system, the app version, whether it is on, and how many threats, files in quarantine and decoy files it has. No file locations and no event details.
  • The app also contacts Supabase about every 15 seconds, which shows your IP address and when you are active.
  • Commands you send to a computer, such as a quick scan, with a short result.
  • The summary and the commands stay after you sign out or remove the app, until you remove the computer under Devices or the project owner deletes the account. You cannot delete your account in the app yet.

Pictures

  • For your profile photo, the app asks Gravatar, a picture service, using a scrambled code of your email. Gravatar sees that code and your IP address.
  • If you paste a wallpaper link, the websites it comes from see your IP address.
