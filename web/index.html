What it does

Zip-bomb inspection
Archives are checked before they are extracted: overlapping entries, sizes that lie, nested archives and Zip64.
New executable scoring
An ONNX model on your device scores new unsigned executables. Every verdict shows the score, the threshold and the reasons in plain text.
Canary files
Decoy files sit in your folders. If something changes them the way ransomware does, The Nex alerts you.
Process guard
Watches for suspicious scripts being run.
Quarantine with restore
Flagged files are moved aside, never deleted on their own. Restore any of them in one click.
Menu bar and Quick Scan
On Mac it lives in the menu bar and runs a Quick Scan from there.

How it is different

Most protection tools send files or hashes to a vendor for a verdict. The Nex does not. The model, the archive checks and the canaries all run locally, and protection keeps working with no account and no network.

Every detection explains itself. You see the score, the threshold it was compared with and the reasons, so you can judge a verdict instead of trusting it.

On applications the model never saw in training, 0.46% of clean programs were flagged. The malware side of the model was trained on synthetic profiles of documented malware traits, so that number says how often real software is wrongly flagged. It is not a detection rate, and there is no lab certification.

An optional account lets you see all your computers and run a Quick Scan on another one. The Nex also includes AegisMesh, a simulated SOS wearable demo. It is a simulation only.

Privacy

This website sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, runs no analytics and makes no third-party requests unless you send a problem report. The app does not scan in the cloud and does not upload files. If you sign in, a small device summary is synced through Supabase. The full details are on the privacy page.
